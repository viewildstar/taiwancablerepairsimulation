Tiles whose source line is in brass are derived or assumed, not measured. The global fault count, the regional share, the repair-capable share of the world fleet and the 40-day mean are published. How many repair hulls a given maintenance zone holds on standby is not disclosed by anyone — club membership and priority position are commercially confidential, which is itself one of the findings. The zone fleet here is calibrated so that server utilisation lands between 0.70 and 0.85; everything downstream of it, including faults per hull per year, inherits that assumption.

Restoration time splits into waiting for a vessel, sailing to the fault, and working on site. Only the last two are physical. Waiting is an allocation: repair is carried out by cooperative maintenance agreements that hold ships on standby and ration by contract seniority when faults coincide. Taiwan's domestic island cables are shallow and short, so they are the fastest to repair and among the slowest to be restored.

Fault locations, cable inventory and severity classes follow the Ministry of Digital Affairs FY114 submarine cable damage analysis (March 2026, released CC0) and MODA's live fault table. Parameters are calibrated, not estimated — this is an illustration of a mechanism. Model, data and assumptions: github.com/viewildstar/taiwancablerepairsimulation
